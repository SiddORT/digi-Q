# Superadmin permanent user deletion — approval plan

## Status and authorization

**Approved for implementation; not implemented.** The user approved this revised plan, including the existing visit-based replacement-doctor access rule, preservation of clinical document contents and the postcommit personal-file-cleanup-pending boundary. This document records a source-code inventory and proposed design, not a working deletion feature or a production-data audit. Approval is not authorization to delete real accounts or deploy.

The requested data boundary is **user-owned data only**, with explicit, reviewed handoff of operational responsibility before deletion. Delete the staff login account and exclusively personal data. Preserve clinics, branches, patient profiles, appointments, clinical documents, shared operational history and other staff accounts. A handoff must not rewrite historical care or silently change a patient's clinician. Never reinterpret the existing resource DELETE endpoints: those remain deactivation operations for their existing scoped roles. Native PostgreSQL authentication remains authoritative, including database revocation for native and optional JWT cookie modes.

Implementation may proceed. All implementation validation must use disposable fixtures, stubbed delivery and isolated storage, never production/UAT records or live provider sends.

### 1a. Role-specific handoff before deletion

The deletion review shows **what depends on this person**, with exact row counts, safe names/scopes and direct resolution links. A transfer is an explicit, separately reviewed operation; merely choosing a destination in the deletion dialog cannot authorize deletion or silently apply changes. After any handoff, reload the impact and require the word `delete` again. There is no automatic ownership transfer.

| Target | Warning and offered resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Doctor (including a consulting Clinic Admin) | Show counts for patients with current/future care relationships, upcoming and overdue unresolved visits, pending guest requests, active consultation/presence, schedules, doctor-specific links and branch hours following that doctor. Offer **Transfer future care to an existing doctor** or **Create a new doctor, then transfer**. A new doctor is created through the ordinary verified creation/invitation path, not as an empty placeholder during deletion. Destination must be active, valid for each clinic/branch, and have valid availability/capacity for any moved booking. Let Superadmin review each patient and visit destination; do not infer a current care assignment from all historical visits. Patient profile, documents and completed/cancelled/no-show visits remain unchanged. If the product has no explicit longitudinal patient-to-doctor assignment for a patient, do not fabricate or move one: transfer only identified open care obligations and offer an explicit new care assignment with patient/clinic authorization rules if such a relationship is introduced. Any eligible booked visit being moved needs an atomic rebooking preserving patient and clinic, with a new valid token/session and explicit before/after review; never rewrite the original doctor, token, reference or audit history in place. Record a linked transition and issue appropriate future-visit notifications under existing consent/notification rules; suppress stale reminders. Pending guest requests need a safe reviewed reassignment or rejection and re-request, preserving receipts. Overdue or in-progress visits require individual clinical/operational resolution first. |
| Clinic Admin | Show owned clinics, legacy owner links, branches with linked consulting hours, managed doctors and receptionists (including inactive), and any self-owned doctor care obligations. Offer **Transfer responsibilities to an existing eligible Clinic Admin** or **Create a new Clinic Admin, then transfer**. Recheck clinic scope, exactly-one-admin ownership and assignment constraints; transfer each clinic and dependent staff together under the established ownership guards, preserving their accounts and care records. Resolve consulting-doctor care as above. Do not move an entire clinic to a doctor or receptionist. |
| Receptionist | Show active assignments, unfinished queue/booking actions and delegated responsibility if any. Offer a reviewed handoff of open operational work to an eligible assigned staff member where a real ownership relation exists, otherwise resolve it individually; preserve appointments and action history. Removing only this person's assignment is not a patient transfer. |
| Superadmin | Show protected-account/last-active-Superadmin warning, plus any actual clinic or doctor responsibilities; never self-delete or remove the last active Superadmin. Hand off real responsibilities to an eligible role first. |

Do not represent every patient who ever visited a doctor as “their patient,” grant another clinician access to an entire medical record through deletion, or move shared history merely because it references the target. The current schema links patients to clinics/branches and appointments to doctors; it does **not** contain a general patient-to-doctor roster. Therefore the first implementation must identify open care by actual visit/request rows, not claim to bulk-transfer a patient roster. A future longitudinal assignment, if desired, requires a separate consent and access design. Handoffs need role/scope authorization, patient privacy checks, availability and conflicts, audited old/new responsibility, idempotent requests, stale-review rejection and independent PostgreSQL race tests. If a chosen destination cannot safely take every selected item, the handoff fails atomically with per-item reasons; no partial silent migration. The impacted deletion manifest must be refreshed afterwards.

**Source-check findings that constrain this handoff:**

1. The ordinary reschedule operation changes an appointment's doctor/session/token **in place**, while appending prior coordinates to its history. It also enforces a cancellation cutoff and cannot reschedule checked-in visits. A deletion handoff must not bypass those protections or rely on the mutated current row alone as an immutable record of the old clinician. Design a linked old/new appointment transition (or a proven equivalent immutable historical projection) with exactly-once queue/token and notification effects, retaining the original receipt and audit trail. Do not call an in-place update “preservation of the original visit” without verifying every report, ticket and notification reader.
2. A doctor can currently read a patient through **any** matching historical appointment; document clinic access also derives from visits. **User-selected handoff rule:** keep the existing visit-based access policy. Once the replacement doctor has a valid handed-off visit, they receive the same patient-profile and same-clinic document access as any doctor with a visit; they gain no cross-clinic access. The old doctor's historical visit may continue to confer their existing access until account removal, after which their account cannot sign in. No general patient-roster transfer or blanket document sharing is implied. Require a pre/post privacy matrix for patient profile, activity and document list/download/upload for the old and new doctor, before and after handoff/deletion, preserving patient-facing privacy. Do not introduce a narrower visit-only policy as an incidental deletion change.
3. Changing a clinic's admin currently checks assigned staff compatibility and updates admin assignments through database guards; it is not sufficient to set `clinics.admin_id` alone. A new Clinic Admin can be created pending ownership transfer; they must be active/eligible before accepting it. Transfer dependent doctor and receptionist ownership/scope in a safe order within the reviewed transaction, including inactive dependents, and verify the exact-one-owner/assignment invariants. `clinics.owner_id` is set from the creator and persists on ordinary admin changes; classify whether each occurrence is historical attribution before anonymizing it, never blindly redirecting it to the replacement.
4. Booking creation currently stores `doctorName`, reference and token in appointment JSON; rescheduling, notification snapshots and clinical activity reads have separate projections. Anonymize only target-derived application attribution, including previous-session and history fields; preserve patient-generated clinical text, document bytes and immutable care evidence. Detect failure to update *any* reader or writer through the reference registry, not only foreign keys.

Proposed protected handoff API: a Superadmin-only `POST /management/system-users/:id/handoff-impact` accepts proposed destination IDs and selected open-care/responsibility IDs and returns a short-lived exact-row review (no writes). `POST /management/system-users/:id/handoff` accepts that review token and an idempotent request ID, reauthorizes and rechecks locks, eligibility, every affected row and scheduling capacity inside one transaction, and returns an auditable operation receipt. Bad roles, stale selections, incomplete coverage or in-progress care reject without writes. New destinations must be created through existing authorized account flows before requesting a handoff review. Do not use this endpoint to grant blanket record access. Separate handoff from deletion so the operator can inspect the new state and explicitly approve the deletion afresh.

**Consistency gate:** Build a reverse-reference registry spanning SQL foreign keys and constraints, application JSON/encrypted snapshots, settings keys, media locators, read projections, generated downloads and cached names. For each target reference mark **transferred**, **anonymized but preserved**, **exclusively removed**, **intentionally retained clinical content**, or **blocked/unclassified**. Reject deletion if any blocked/unclassified reference remains. Assert before commit and again after mutation that every surviving required relationship points to an eligible live account or an explicitly retired anonymous identity, that no operational pointer targets a retired doctor/deleted account, that shared record counts/content and unrelated accounts are unchanged, and that exclusive-storage cleanup has a durable receipt. After commit, re-read through actual staff/patient/public endpoints and compare with the manifest; a failed verification must be visible and actionable rather than reported as clean completion. Run this against disposable relational/storage fixtures; a successful FK check alone is insufficient for JSON, queues, session credentials or notifications.

## 2. Anonymous history and schema proposal

Use **nullable identity references plus explicit deleted-identity state**, not a synthetic users row and never a real replacement actor.

1. Allow null creator/actor/uploader references on retained appointments, appointment history, documents and shared saved views. Existing nullable audit/guest references need explicit deletion attribution to distinguish “Deleted user” from a system/unknown actor.
2. Add a retired/deleted identity marker to doctors, permit a null login reference only in that state, and enforce inactive/no-operational-assignment invariants. Keep ordinary active-doctor ownership requirements. If a consulting admin retires their self-owned profile, allow its owner link to be detached only for that retired profile; other managed profiles remain blockers.
3. Amend the existing deferred ownership functions/guards narrowly for retired profiles. Preserve exactly-one-clinic-admin rules and ordinary active doctor/staff validation. Add migrations; do not rewrite historical migrations, disable triggers globally or use broad cascades.
4. Update SQL projections and object enrichment together. `enrich("doctors")` currently requires a user lookup; several history/notification projections join users directly. Retired doctor reads must succeed without that account and return **“Deleted doctor”**; explicit deleted actor/uploader reads return **“Deleted user.”** Preserve existing role privacy rules (do not expose actors to patients where currently hidden).
5. Staff/public doctor directories and selected-ID hydration must exclude retired identities from eligibility, even with explicit inactive/ID filters. Historical appointment/report filters need a separate anonymous historical projection so retained visits remain readable.
6. Sanitize authoritative doctor/actor snapshots at known application JSON paths, including rendered notification text and current/previous confirmation snapshots. Do not globally replace names across patient medical notes: that can corrupt clinical content or change another person's name.

**Clinical-content limitation:** a clinician's signed name inside a preserved uploaded PDF or medical narrative is part of the retained clinical document, not merely an uploader/doctor reference. The plan preserves that content. The confirmation must not promise those bytes are scrubbed. If the requirement is literal erasure of every name inside clinical content, it conflicts with preserving documents unchanged and requires a separate user decision before implementation. Application-generated attribution, filenames derived from the staff profile and metadata remain in scope for anonymization.

## 3. Proposed protected API contract

Introduce dedicated routes under the existing Superadmin-only management namespace; do not repurpose `DELETE /users/:id` or `DELETE /doctors/:id`.

- `GET /management/system-users/:id/deletion-impact`: authenticated active Superadmin/session proof, `Cache-Control: no-store`. Return target identity/role/status, eligibility, safe blockers with resolution guidance, counts by remove/anonymize/preserve category, verified exclusive-storage counts, exclusions and a short-lived opaque review token. Do not return credentials, storage keys, recipient contacts or decrypted messages.
- `POST /management/system-users/:id/permanent-delete`: CSRF-protected, strict body `{ confirmation: "delete", reviewToken, requestId }`. Exact equality without case folding, trimming or coercion. Authorize again inside the transaction; a revoked/inactive/demoted actor cannot rely on earlier middleware approval.
- `GET /management/user-deletions/:operationId`: Superadmin-only receipt/status for ambiguous responses or storage cleanup pending. Minimize stored receipt metadata; no deleted full name/email/mobile/legacy provider ID or credentials.

Review tokens are actor/target scoped, server-generated and expire; they are not authorization. Bind them to a canonical manifest of exact affected rows, revisions/content hashes, blocker evidence, **completed handoff receipts and destination eligibility**, and exclusive file references, not just category totals. Two different records can have equal counts. Bind target profile/role/status and last-active-admin/ownership eligibility as well. Recompute under locking before write; return `409 DELETION_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and clear confirmation on any changed impact or eligibility. Counts and labels must come from the same fresh snapshot.

Suggested error categories: `DELETION_SELF_FORBIDDEN`, `DELETION_LAST_SUPERADMIN`, `DELETION_OWNERSHIP_BLOCKED`, `DELETION_DOCTOR_WORK_BLOCKED`, `DELETION_IMPACT_UNAVAILABLE`, `DELETION_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `DELETION_ALREADY_IN_PROGRESS`. Return actionable safe text, not raw database/provider errors. Anonymous actors receive 401; authenticated non-Superadmins receive 403 before seeing impact.

Store one receipt per actor/request/target binding. Same request replays only that operation/status; a different request after deletion returns an explicit already-removed/not-found response with no new cleanup. Reject request-ID reuse for another target. Receipt retention must not recreate a private identity directory.

## 4. Atomicity, races and delivery boundary

### Database operation

Acquire a documented common lock order and revalidate the current actor/session, target and impact. Reuse `super-admin-protection` for all deletion/deactivation checks affecting active Superadmins, the `auth-credentials:<id>` boundary for login/reset/invitation issuance, and assignment/doctor/schedule/queue locks as appropriate. Lock target and actor rows and shared JSON rows before mutation.

Advisory locks only protect cooperating writers. Add narrow database-backed retired/deletion guards and make affected writers participate: profile edits, ownership transfers, assignment creation, doctor booking/guest capture, queue actions, schedule edits, logo uploads, custom-role saves, native session/challenge creation and notification preparation/dispatch. Foreign keys alone do not prevent a new booking against a preserved retired doctor. Serializable transactions with bounded serialization/deadlock handling are an additional protection, not a replacement for these guards. A retry must recompute the manifest and must never silently accept a newly broadened impact.

In one database transaction:

1. Reject self-delete, patient targets, final active Superadmin, responsibilities and unresolved doctor work; a displayed but uncommitted handoff is not sufficient.
2. Verify the exact confirmation and reviewed manifest; reject stale review without writes.
3. Stop target-specific pending delivery and sanitize shared actor/doctor/recipient data.
4. Delete only exclusive auth/challenge/proof/assignment/read-marker/preferences/private-activity rows and remove custom-role bindings.
5. Retire the linked doctor, remove exclusive availability and doctor booking links, detach retained actor/uploader/patient-account links, and anonymize historical application metadata.
6. Check the reverse-reference registry for unresolved operational and unclassified relationships, then physically delete the user account. Write a minimal anonymous deletion audit and operation receipt/file-cleanup manifest. Validate post-mutation relational and application-reference invariants before commit.

Any precommit error rolls back all database changes. Cleanup operations must not swallow integrity errors.

### Notification and authentication races

Current email/SMS workers commit a `sending` claim before sending, then write a completion snapshot from memory. Merely deleting pending rows cannot stop an in-flight send and that completion can restore erased identity data.

Implement a per-recipient dispatch barrier that spans claim through provider completion and is also acquired by deletion; all sends to staff, including invitation/reset sends and notification test delivery, must use it. Lock ordering must avoid credential/dispatch deadlocks. Obtain bounded exclusive barriers before cleanup, recheck current account and operation state immediately before dispatch, and prevent late worker completions from restoring private snapshots. Use conditional updates against current operation/revision state, not unconditional replacement from stale memory.

If dispatch is active or an uncertain claim cannot be safely reconciled, block deletion with guidance rather than claiming no later delivery. A provider-accepted message may reach an inbox after deletion and cannot be recalled; disclose this limitation. Native/JWT sessions, setup/reset/device challenges and legacy proofs must all be ineffective after commit, and concurrently issued credentials must serialize with deletion.

### External file cleanup: explicit two-phase result

PostgreSQL cannot atomically roll back local/object storage deletion. Do not remove bytes before database commit: a rollback could otherwise lose a shared file. Do not use the existing `removeDocument` behavior for a permanent-erasure worker because it suppresses all storage errors.

- First commit account removal/anonymization with a durable, restricted, exact exclusive-file cleanup manifest.
- Then clean only verified exclusive assets through strict removal helpers: verified not-found is idempotent success; permission, network and filesystem errors are retryable failures.
- Return **completed** only when exclusive storage cleanup is verified. If it fails after commit, return **account removed; personal file cleanup pending**, not a generic “failed, unchanged” response and not unqualified success. Block access immediately; retries resume the same operation rather than repeating deletion.
- Manifest status must expose safe counts/errors to Superadmins, not raw paths or identity. Retain restricted storage locators only until cleanup completes. Conditional completion writes must not reintroduce erased JSON.
- Preserve patient-document bytes and shared logos regardless of uploader. Outstanding signed upload URLs may remain usable until expiry: disable application finalization, track expiry and recheck exclusive object cleanup after that window before claiming completion. External HTTPS assets are excluded.

This explicitly separates database rollback failures (unchanged account/data) from postcommit storage failures (account already removed, cleanup visibly pending); the UI must not promise an impossible cross-system rollback.

## 5. Confirmation and entry points

Use one shared deletion action/dialog component from System users and existing Superadmin staff listings/drawers, including embedded Clinic Staff screens. Doctor rows resolve their linked account ID; do not send a doctor ID to the user endpoint. Other roles receive no action and remain forbidden at the API. Retired historical doctors receive no edit/invite/schedule/delete-account action.

The distinct labeled destructive action is **Delete user**, not the activation switch or existing generic Delete/deactivation action. Its dialog:

- Identifies the freshly loaded target by name, email and role.
- Loads impact on every open; loading/error/blocked states cannot submit. Retry reloads the review and clears typed confirmation.
- Separates **Removed**, **Anonymized**, **Preserved**, **Cannot erase externally**, and **Must resolve first** sections with server counts and categories, plus a role-specific responsibility/relationship summary.
- Offers reviewed **Transfer to an existing eligible staff member** and **Create new staff member, then transfer** links appropriate to the target's role. New staff creation and handoff each require their own authorization/review; return to a freshly loaded deletion impact afterwards. For doctors, distinguish patients with open care from historical patients, and list appointments that cannot be automatically moved. For Clinic Admins, enumerate owned clinics and dependent staff. Never suggest that selecting a destination will silently move clinical content or make deletion possible before transfer commits.
- Explains permanent loss of login access, no undo, no automatic ownership transfer and no deletion of patient visits. An approved future booking transfer is an explicit rebooking with a preserved history trail, not deletion of the original appointment.
- Requires the exact lowercase word `delete` in a labeled input; reject `Delete`, `DELETE`, spaces, other words and empty input on client and server. Never auto-fill it.
- Clears confirmation and requires fresh review when target/impact changes or after a conflict. Do not automatically submit after refreshing.
- Uses focus trapping, descriptive accessible title, error/status announcements, initial focus on safe/cancel controls, keyboard operation, visible disabled state, mobile scrolling and focus return to the trigger.
- Cancel/Escape before submission causes no deletion. During submission prevent double clicks, Enter re-entry and misleading cancellation; an ambiguous response offers Check status rather than submitting a new operation.

Suggested warning: “This permanently removes this staff account and the personal data listed below. They lose login access immediately when account removal commits. Shared clinics, patients, appointments and clinical documents remain, with application attribution shown as Deleted user or Deleted doctor. Review and complete any listed care or ownership transfers first; nothing transfers automatically. There is no undo. Already-delivered messages, external-provider logs, backups and previously downloaded files are not erased; preserved clinical documents are not rewritten. Personal file cleanup can remain pending after account removal.”

## 6. Refresh and privacy verification

On database removal, cancel outstanding target reads and purge target identity caches before invalidation so stale results cannot repopulate labels. Refresh system/staff lists and counts, role bindings, assignments, effective permissions, exact drawers, search results, ResourceLookup/CareLookup/selected-label caches, public discovery/QR contexts, notifications, patient documents/history, appointments/reports/ticket generation. Keep current filters and clamp empty pages.

Deletion receipts may show pending cleanup, but staff directories must stop displaying the removed account as soon as account removal commits. Historical visits/documents remain accessible within unchanged permissions with anonymous attribution. Verify both direct enrichment and SQL projections; sanitizing one response path does not protect downloads or notification histories. Already-open clients need refreshed server results and a deletion invalidation signal/polling; do not claim offline/browser-downloaded copies are remotely erased.

## 7. Implementation sequence after approval

1. Complete the reverse-reference registry and strict impact classifier; freeze API/manifest/error semantics, role-specific handoff rules and the clinical-content/storage limitations above.
2. Implement separately reviewed, audited, atomic role-appropriate handoff paths for existing or newly created eligible destinations, including conflict-safe future appointment rebooking where supported; verify historical data is unchanged. Add the needed no-orphan guards and migration-fixture tests.
3. Add additive schema/trigger changes and historical read projections, with migration-fixture tests for active ownership invariants and anonymous doctor reads.
4. Add dispatch/credential/write barriers and retirement checks to all affected writers before enabling deletion routes.
5. Implement atomic cleanup, strict external cleanup tracking, exact review checks and replay/status recovery; update OpenAPI and generated client/schema packages.
6. Add the shared accessible dialog/action and role-specific handoff links across Superadmin entry points, then dependent-cache invalidation.
7. Execute the full destructive-boundary suite below on isolated data; keep the action disabled until all mandatory acceptance checks pass.

No automatic transfer, bulk deletion, patient deletion, undo, deployment or real-data cleanup belongs in these steps.
